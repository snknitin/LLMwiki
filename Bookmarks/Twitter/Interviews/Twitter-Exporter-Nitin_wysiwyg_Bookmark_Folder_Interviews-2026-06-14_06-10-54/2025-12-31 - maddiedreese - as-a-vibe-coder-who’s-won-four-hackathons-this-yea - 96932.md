# As a vibe coder who’s won four hackathons this year, here’s how I find hackathons to compete in:

> **Source:** [@maddiedreese](https://x.com/maddiedreese) · 2025-12-31 · 👍 785 · 💬 54 · 🔖 956 · 👁 40685

> 🔗 [View tweet on X](https://x.com/maddiedreese/status/2006488738898296932)

## Tweet Content

As a vibe coder who’s won four hackathons this year, here’s how I find hackathons to compete in:

X. Most hackathons are posted here on X. At least once a week, I do targeted searches including visiting the profiles of all major vibe coding tools. That covers most of them. Then, I also search for the following keywords on all of X: “vibe coding” “vibe coders” “hackathon” “vibe coding competition” “vibe coding contest” “ai coding.” This combined effort pulls up a decent amount of the ones that have been posted.


@contra
. Sign up for a Contra account. They’re always running fun challenges with great sponsors. I just won second place ($2,500) at one last night!


@Reddit
. The subreddits /r/vibecoding /r/vibecodersnest /r/hackathon and /r/vibecodedevs often have smaller competitions being advertised through posts. I found a competition that I won 1st place and $1,000 through from a post in one of those subreddits.

Web search. Usually Google. Keywords like “vibe coding hackathon” “vibe coding competition” “vibe coding contest” etc. all work well. Don’t forget to use Boolean operators to narrow down your search parameters!

Community. I am very grateful to have a lot of great friends on here who tag me in hackathons 

Good luck, and let me know if you find any cool hackathons to compete in!
