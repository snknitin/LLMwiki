## Why Memory Chip Stocks Are Falling (It's Not the Google paper)

Memory chip stock bulls are blaming a Google Research paper for the recent drop, but that's not why the shares are falling.

The real reason is much simpler: prices for certain smartphone memory chips stopped rising. Buyers finally said “No”—the first sign seasoned memory-cycle investors look for before they sell.

Some smartphone makers now plan fewer (or zero) mid-to-low-end handsets in 2026. High DRAM and NAND prices make them too expensive.

Word came to me through a friend.

I was furious.

“My guy called two weeks ago and said some buyers have refused higher DDR4 prices recently,” he said.

Me: “Two weeks ago?!?”

“Well, that’s why I called you for coffee.”

Me: “I called you!”

Since then, memory chip stocks have pulled back. Micron is down over 24% from its recent high of $471.34. SanDisk is off nearly 21% from $777.60.

Investors following the memory-cycle playbook got out fast. The industry (DRAM and NAND) is notoriously cyclical.

There have been about a dozen major boom/bust cycles in memory chips over the past 50 years, and at least three since 2010:

· 2012–2015: Smartphones (3G/4G transition + social media explosion). Cloud supports with data centers. Smartphones overtake PCs as the biggest memory consumer.

· 2016–2019: Cloud/hyperscaler expansion + smartphones (storage upgrades, 5G coming).

· 2020–2023: COVID-driven surge in PC/server demand for work-from-home. Cloud providers become the biggest memory buyers.

· 2024–202?: AI-driven upcycle—server memory focused on training (HBM) and inference (SRAM).

The cycles are so common they’re called “hog cycles” after farming.

High prices cause farmers to raise more pigs, but breeding takes time—so it’s a year before the new pork hits the market. When it does – all at once – prices plunge.

In DRAM/NAND, the “breeding time” is the long wait to build a new fab.

Companies rush to expand when prices are high, but when everyone ramps output at once, the new supply crashes prices again.

When prices recover, the cycle repeats—in memory chips just like hogs.

Memory-cycle investors follow their playbook. It doesn’t matter how many people write “This Time Is Different”—that’s one of the classic signs bullish sentiment has gone wild.

“Markets are never wrong—opinions often are.” — Jesse Livermore (Reminiscences of a Stock Operator, 1923)

Is this the top?

Longer term, there are reasons to believe this memory-chip cycle will last longer than others. The AI demand surge is real, and the buildout continues despite Wall Street skepticism.

The beginning is always rocketing chip prices that fuel stock prices higher. Chip buyers appear to have finally stopped panicking.

The mini-selloff from Google’s TurboQuant paper had less to do with the findings than what it represents: Everyone is hunting for solutions to high memory-chip prices.

https://x.com/RajaXg/status/2037622039314670076

He’s right. High DRAM and NAND prices make new ideas worth chasing.

Memory investors may find their next great ideas in more R&D-focused companies. On-chip memory, for example, is hot right now.

Nvidia paid $20 billion for Groq’s inference technology and key team members. The resulting Nvidia Groq 3 LPU chip (made on Samsung’s 4nm process) packs ~500 MB of on-chip SRAM—among the most on any conventional-size die—with extreme 150 TB/s bandwidth for faster inference (quicker answers from Grok, Gemini, or ChatGPT).

And there’s work underway with stacked wafers and other methods for even more on-chip SRAM in future generations.

That’s not all.

Newer memory technologies like ReRAM or MRAM could one day help by offering more on-chip storage capacity, while still being quick like SRAM. Because they don’t lose data when power is off and they run more efficiently, they could give chips extra fast memory without taking up as much valuable space or draining as much power as pure SRAM does today.

These are only a few examples of memory that might work better in the AI era. AI firms are willing to pay top dollar for performance and energy efficiency.

The wide moat DRAM and NAND have enjoyed for so long—the lowest price-per-bit—matters less when prices rise so high and shortages loom.

So, is the memory boom over?

Doubtful.

It looks like the memory-chip boom is still early and may run longer than a normal cycle. The “supercycle” tag fits because of the scale of the AI data-center buildout.

Memory stocks may well catch a second wind.

But seasoned hog-cycle investors will always follow the playbook.

And memory stocks will have to prove themselves. Pay attention to how they behave. If rebounds are met with persistent selling, wait and let them set up again.
