# Most folks are still using AI like a slightly smarter search box.

> **Source:** [@tonysimons_](https://x.com/tonysimons_) · 2026-07-01 · 👍 234 · 💬 14 · 🔖 693 · 👁 48354

> 🔗 [View tweet on X](https://x.com/tonysimons_/status/2072435248382591375)

## Article Content

Most folks are still using AI like a slightly smarter search box.

They ask it to summarize something, rewrite an email, brainstorm a few ideas, or make a paragraph sound less weird.

That’s useful, sure. But it’s also the shallow end of the pool.

The real jump happens when you stop treating AI like a chat window and start treating it like an operator.

Give it tools. Give it files. Give it memory. Give it schedules. Let it work across the browser, terminal, inbox, calendar, GitHub, WordPress, smart home, or whatever else you actually use every day.

That’s where [Hermes Agent](https://github.com/NousResearch/hermes-agent)

 gets interesting.

Hermes is an open-source agent framework built for real workflows, not just one-off prompts. Research, monitoring, publishing, coding, alerts, automation, competitive intelligence, personal productivity, and all the annoying connective tissue between apps that usually eats your afternoon. It’s the difference between asking AI for advice and giving AI a job.

So without further adieu, here are 20 out-of-the-box Hermes Agent workflows worth building, stealing, remixing, or immediately putting on your own roadmap.

Some are practical. Some are weird. Some are the kind of thing that makes basic chatbot usage feel tiny once you understand what’s possible.

Let’s get into it.

### 1. The Murder Board

https://x.com/tonysimons_/article/2072435248382591375/media/2072161813144256512

Everybody has the folder. Bookmarks, screenshots, PDFs, X posts, half-finished notes, saved links, random “come back to this later” junk from a research rabbit hole that started with one question and ended with 47 tabs open.

The Murder Board workflow is simple: feed that whole mess to Hermes and have it turn the pile into a map. Who said what? When did they say it? Which sources agree? Which ones contradict each other? Which details actually matter, and which ones just felt important at the time because you were three coffees deep and chasing a thread?

This is one of the cleanest ways to feel the difference between basic AI and agentic work. Basic AI answers a question. Hermes can help make sense of the mess around the question. That matters because serious work almost never starts clean.

Research, product reviews, investigations, technical debugging, content planning, competitive analysis, buying decisions. The inputs are usually scattered across files, tabs, screenshots, notes, and half-dead ideas. Hermes can walk into the junk drawer with a flashlight and come back with a usable map.

### 2. The Competitor Autopsy

https://x.com/tonysimons_/article/2072435248382591375/media/2072170780457103360

Point Hermes at a competitor’s website, GitHub, changelog, docs, pricing page, and X account, then have it run a structured teardown. What are they shipping? How fast are they moving? What are users complaining about? What features are they quietly burying? Where does the pricing look soft? What’s their positioning really saying?

The goal is not some sleepy “top 5 alternatives” SEO puddle. The goal is an actual briefing with evidence links, screenshots, page diffs, release history, user complaints, and a clear summary of where the opportunity is.

Most companies either don’t do competitive intelligence at all, or they pay for a dashboard that slowly turns into digital wallpaper. Hermes can turn the same work into an operator task: check the sources, extract the signals, show the receipts, and tell me what matters.

That’s the useful version. Not “they have a nicer homepage.” More like: “They raised prices, stopped mentioning this feature, users are complaining about support, and they just hired two people for integrations.” That’s information you can use.

### 3. The Price Prophet

https://x.com/tonysimons_/article/2072435248382591375/media/2072171705762545665

Pick a product you want, then have Hermes check the current price, historical price trackers, cached pages, manufacturer promos, and deal history. The point isn’t just to ask, “Is this on sale?” That’s amateur hour.

The better question is whether it’s actually a real deal. Has it been cheaper? Is the discount fake? Is this the normal price wearing a party hat? Should you buy now, or wait because the pattern says it’ll probably drop again soon?

Everybody has bought something and watched it drop $80 three days later. That specific little act of financial violence is what this workflow exists to prevent.

The best version gives you a recommendation with proof: buy now, wait, ignore this fake discount, or jump because it’s the lowest price in six months. Price alerts are everywhere. Price alerts with actual evidence are much more useful.

### 4. The Meeting Prep Autopilot

https://x.com/tonysimons_/article/2072435248382591375/media/2072172649522814976

Before a meeting, have Hermes research the person or company you’re talking to. Recent posts, company news, LinkedIn context, past emails, mutual connections, previous notes, relevant projects, whatever gives you actual context before you click Join.

Then have it send you a briefing card before the call. Who are they? What do they care about? What changed recently? What should you ask? What should you avoid? Where’s the actual opening for a useful conversation?

After the meeting, Hermes can help draft the follow-up based on your notes and your writing style. That’s where it starts to compound. One prepared meeting is nice. Every meeting being prepped, tracked, and followed up on properly is leverage.

This is one of those workflows that sounds small until you imagine it running quietly across your whole calendar. Less scrambling, less context switching, fewer “wait, who is this again?” moments five seconds before the call starts.

### 5. The Daily Opponent

https://x.com/tonysimons_/article/2072435248382591375/media/2072173499603410944

This one is nasty in the best way. Give Hermes your current idea, project, thesis, article draft, product plan, or take, then tell it to attack the thing properly.

Not lazy devil’s advocate stuff. Not “have you considered both sides?” theater. The useful version finds the strongest counterargument, the best contradictory evidence, the assumption you’re making too casually, and the part of your logic that’s probably weakest.

Most AI tools are tuned to agree with you because agreement feels nice. This workflow makes the agent useful by making it disagree well. That’s a much better test of whether your idea can survive contact with reality.

Smart people are extremely good at building padded rooms out of their own opinions. A daily opponent keeps the walls from getting too soft.

### 6. The 3AM Watchdog

https://x.com/tonysimons_/article/2072435248382591375/media/2072174302120615936

Some things need to be watched, but you should not be the one watching them. A product page, a GitHub issue, a job posting, a government form, a release page, a checkout page, a competitor changelog. All of that is perfect agent work.

Set Hermes to check on a schedule and only alert you when something meaningful changes. That last part is the whole game. Keyword alerts are dumb because they fire constantly, train you to ignore them, and eventually become another noisy inbox you resent.

The useful version checks whether the thing you actually care about moved. Did the form open? Did the price drop? Did the status change? Did the repo tag a release? Did the page copy change in a way that matters?

Set the patrol route once and let Hermes walk the fence.

### 7. The Content Decay Detector

https://x.com/tonysimons_/article/2072435248382591375/media/2072175933276950528

Point Hermes at your site and have it find the articles that are losing traffic, slipping in search, or quietly becoming outdated. Then have it explain why. Maybe the links are broken. Maybe the screenshots are old. Maybe the product changed names. Maybe newer competitors answered the search intent better. Maybe the article is still good, but the metadata is weak and the structure needs work.

The important part is ranking the update queue by effort versus upside. This is massive for anyone publishing content because most people chase new posts forever while old winners sit in the basement leaking traffic through the floorboards.

Hermes can help find the leaks. The output you want is simple: update these five articles first, here’s why they’re slipping, here’s what to change, and here’s what the upside probably looks like.

That turns “I should update old content someday” into an actual recovery plan.

### 8. The Competitor Alert System

https://x.com/tonysimons_/article/2072435248382591375/media/2072176607414951936

This is the scheduled version of the competitor autopsy. Have Hermes monitor competitor pricing pages, feature pages, changelogs, docs, landing pages, and job boards, then alert you when something changes.

The useful version doesn’t just say, “A page changed.” It screenshots the page, diffs the copy, highlights what moved, and explains why it might matter. If a competitor raises prices, removes a feature, changes positioning, rewrites their homepage, or starts hiring for a suspiciously specific role, you want to know early.

Again, the goal is not another dashboard. Dashboards are where useful information goes to become furniture. The better version is a direct alert with the screenshot, the diff, and the reason it deserves your attention.

That’s the difference between monitoring and intelligence.

### 9. The Deal Radar

https://x.com/tonysimons_/article/2072435248382591375/media/2072177943942504448

Tell Hermes what product category you care about: portable SSDs, AirPods, OLED TVs, GPUs, robot vacuums, standing desks, whatever. Then have it monitor deal sites, Amazon, manufacturer pages, and price trackers.

The key is to only alert when something hits a real historical low. Not a fake sale. Not a “was $399, now $299” fairy tale where the thing has sold for $279 every other Tuesday since Easter. A real deal, backed by price history.

This is especially useful if you run product content, affiliate content, shopping roundups, or you just enjoy not getting mugged by fake discounts. Most deal content online is just retail confetti shot out of a cannon.

Hermes can be pickier. That’s the whole point.

### 10. The Article Factory

https://x.com/tonysimons_/article/2072435248382591375/media/2072179656887877632

This is the workflow every publisher should be studying. Give Hermes a topic, then have it research the subject, build a source pack, outline the piece, draft it in your style, check the claims, generate a featured image brief, prep SEO metadata, and stage the article in WordPress.

Then you edit. That part matters because the goal is not to let AI spray raw sludge onto your site. The goal is to make the machine do the boring parts so you can spend your time on taste, judgment, structure, opinion, and the sections that actually make the piece worth reading.

The blank page is expensive. A staged draft with sources, structure, and metadata is a much better starting point.

That’s where AI writing gets useful: not replacing the writer, but moving all the scaffolding into place before the writer shows up with the hammer.

### 11. The “Write Like Me” Ghostwriter

https://x.com/tonysimons_/article/2072435248382591375/media/2072180104319451136

Generic AI writing has a smell. It’s clean, smooth, lifeless, and somehow always sounds like it was rinsed in LinkedIn water.

Hermes can do better if you give it examples of your actual writing. Have it study your sentence rhythm, vocabulary, structure, jokes, transitions, blunt sections, longer explanations, and recurring habits. Then have it draft in that pattern.

The killer version also flags the lines that sound least like you. That’s the part most writing workflows miss. A useful ghostwriter shouldn’t just generate copy. It should tell you where the mask slips.

The fix for bad AI writing isn’t “make it more punchy.” The fix is teaching the agent your fingerprints and making it prove when it’s actually using them.

### 12. The Zillow Sniper

https://x.com/tonysimons_/article/2072435248382591375/media/2072180905037156352

House hunting is browser punishment. Zillow turns normal adults into midnight listing goblins scrolling through homes they can’t afford, neighborhoods they don’t understand, and commute estimates that were apparently calculated by a drunk balloon.

Hermes can turn that scroll into a daily digest. Give it your budget, commute tolerance, non-negotiables, school preferences, neighborhood constraints, and dealbreakers. Then have it scrape listings, filter by actual drive time, check surrounding context, screenshot the ones worth seeing, and send a small daily batch.

Not 84 listings. Three good ones. That’s the trick.

AI doesn’t need to replace a realtor here. It just needs to kill the scroll and keep you from wasting your night looking at houses that fail your own rules.

### 13. The Smart Home Conductor

https://x.com/tonysimons_/article/2072435248382591375/media/2072181691641212928

Home automation is great until you’re 45 minutes deep in app menus trying to make a light bulb behave like it went to therapy. The dream is simple: describe what you want, and the system handles the wiring.

Hermes makes the input human again. “When I’m on a work call, dim the office lights, pause music everywhere, and set my status to busy.” That’s the command. Then Hermes can translate the intent into the actual automation, test it, and help debug it when something breaks.

This is what smart home marketing has promised forever. Tell the system what you want, let it handle the setup, and stop manually stitching together 19 apps, four bridges, three scenes called “Office Focus Final 2,” and one bulb that has chosen litigation.

Hermes is how that starts getting civilized.

### 14. The Resume Assassin

https://x.com/tonysimons_/article/2072435248382591375/media/2072182866574761985

Give Hermes a job posting and a resume, then have it rewrite the bullet points to highlight the exact experience the job description is filtering for without lying. That last part matters. This is optimization, not fan fiction.

Then have it score the original resume against the revised one. The job market is brutal, and most resumes are written for the person who already knows you’re good. That’s not who reads it first.

The first reader is usually an ATS, a recruiter moving too fast, or a hiring manager scanning for signals while half-paying attention. Hermes can help shape the resume around the job so the right experience is impossible to miss.

Before and after. Score difference. Cleaner bullets. Better signal. Less praying that the machine understands nuance.

### 15. The Negotiation Prep Kit

https://x.com/tonysimons_/article/2072435248382591375/media/2072190233613934592

Salary, contract, car price, freelance rate, vendor renewal. Any negotiation gets better when you show up with more than a number and vibes.

Have Hermes prep the negotiation before you walk in. It can research market rates, find comparable deals, build your opening position, predict counterarguments, and give you fallback language for each stage.

Most people lose negotiations before they start because they don’t know the floor, the ceiling, or the pressure points. They know what they want, but they don’t know what the other side is likely to say or how to respond without folding immediately.

Hermes can help you show up with comps, ranges, leverage points, and a plan for what to say when the other side pushes back. You don’t need to become a negotiation goblin. You do need to know the floor before you start talking.

### 16. The Side Project Forensic

https://x.com/tonysimons_/article/2072435248382591375/media/2072433426712481792

Every developer has a graveyard. Old repos, half-built apps, weird experiments, and “this could be something” projects that haven’t been opened since the last ice age

Give Hermes access to the pile and have it score each project. How close is it to shipping? What’s broken? What would it take to finish? Is there a market? Are comparable tools making money? Which one deserves to be resurrected first?

This is a brutally useful workflow because the problem usually isn’t having no ideas. The problem is having 19 unfinished ideas and no clean way to decide which one deserves oxygen.

Hermes can be the coroner and the resurrection committee.

### 17. The Release Manager

https://x.com/tonysimons_/article/2072435248382591375/media/2072433897078394880

Tell Hermes to help ship a new version of a project. It can run the test suite, bump the version, generate the changelog from commits, create the GitHub release, build the package, and publish it.

If something fails, it can tell you what broke and suggest the next fix. This is exactly where agents shine: structured technical work, clear goal, repeatable steps, and plenty of small ways to mess it up manually.

Humans are bad at remembering every release step, especially when the steps are scattered across docs, scripts, package managers, changelog conventions, GitHub releases, and muscle memory. Agents are built for checklists with tools attached.

That’s why “AI shipped my release while I ate lunch” sounds fake until you wire the workflow and watch it happen.

### 18. The RSS Bouncer

https://x.com/tonysimons_/article/2072435248382591375/media/2072434244450652160

RSS is great until it becomes a firehose. Feeds, GitHub repos, release notes, X accounts, newsletters, and news sources can turn into a second internet you now have to manage.

Have Hermes monitor the sources but filter for actual relevance based on what you care about and what you’re currently working on. That’s the difference between a feed reader and an intelligence layer.

Most alert systems eventually become noise. They punish you for paying attention until you stop. A good agent should protect attention, not spend it.

Hermes only needs to tell you when something crosses the line from “new” to “useful.”

### 19. The Blender Scene Monkey

https://x.com/tonysimons_/article/2072435248382591375/media/2072434554934018048

This one is ridiculous in the best way. Describe a 3D scene in plain English, then have Hermes write the Blender Python script, run Blender headless, render the scene, and send back the finished image.

No Blender knowledge. No node editor trench warfare. No tutorial spiral. Just describe the scene and let the machine render.

This is the kind of workflow that makes people stop scrolling because it sounds fake until you show the output. Once it works, it opens a whole new creative lane: featured images, product scenes, weird 3D experiments, visuals for posts, and stuff you’d never manually build because you don’t have time to become a Blender monk.

Hermes turns it into an instruction.

### 20. The Knowledge Archaeologist

https://x.com/tonysimons_/article/2072435248382591375/media/2072435172201435136

This might be the biggest one. Have Hermes search across conversations, files, decisions, notes, and past sessions so you can ask something like, “What was that idea I had about X three months ago?”

The useful version doesn’t give you a vague summary. It pulls up the actual context: the session, the wording, the decision, and what happened next.

That’s memory with receipts. Most AI tools treat every conversation like a disposable napkin. You talk, maybe get something useful, then the context evaporates and you start over next time.

Hermes is built around the idea that knowledge should compound. That changes the relationship completely. You’re not starting from zero every time. You’re building a durable operating layer over your own work.

### The Real Point of All of This

The model isn’t the magic. GPT, Claude, Gemini, local models, hosted models, whatever. They’re all capable enough for a shocking amount of this.

The difference is the operating layer around the model: tools, memory, schedules, files, terminal access, browser access, calendar access, specialized agents, publishing pipelines, and a way to remember what happened yesterday so it can do something useful with it tomorrow.

That’s why Hermes matters. It gives the model hands, memory, routines, and a job description.

Once you experience AI that can operate across your actual environment, basic chatbot usage starts feeling tiny. Still useful, but tiny.

Hermes is open source. It runs locally. You can wire it into the workflows you already use. And if you’re still only using AI to summarize emails, you’re playing with the box it came in.

Pick one workflow. Build it. Then build the next one. 🤘
