# Here’s the dirty secret about AI eval dashboards:

> **Source:** [Paul Iusztin](https://www.linkedin.com/in/ACoAACQFQWgBqowPZBqBQgSC3ATmuatVfZkf6fE) · Senior AI Engineer • Founder @ Decoding AI • Author @ LLM Engineer’s Handbook ~ I ship AI products and teach you about the process. · 11mo

> [View post on LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7374781894523084800/)

## Post

Here’s the dirty secret about AI eval dashboards:

Those pretty 1–5 scores don’t actually tell you anything.

Just think about it...

What does a "3.7" in helpfulness or a "4.2" in truthfulness even mean?

Most teams usually draw a blank when posed this question too.

And this is why Hamel Husain constantly says Likert scales (1–5 ratings) are a trap.

• They’re subjective → one person’s 3 is another’s 4.
• They’re noisy → it takes huge sample sizes to know if you actually improved.
• They’re lazy → annotators default to the middle score just to move on.
 
The result of these evals is dashboards full of numbers that look good, but don’t do anything to inform product improvements.

So how do we fix it?

• Replace fuzzy 1–5 scores with binary, pass/fail checks.
• Define specific failure modes (e.g. constraint violation, hallucinated tool call).
• Track those directly to see what’s breaking and what’s improving.
 
This forces clarity.

It makes labeling faster.

And it gives engineers an actionable signal.
