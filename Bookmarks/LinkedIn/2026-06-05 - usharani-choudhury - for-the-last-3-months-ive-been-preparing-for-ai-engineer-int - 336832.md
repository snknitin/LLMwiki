# For the last 3 months, I've been preparing for AI Engineer interviews almost every day.

> **Source:** [Usharani Choudhury](https://www.linkedin.com/in/ACoAABtueCsBn7CNF9nuU1pEZCnR-NpXwJfKTXI) · Forward Deployed Engineer | Agentic AI | RAG & LangGraph | GenAI Platforms | Production LLM Systems | AWS · 2mo

> [View post on LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7468512224425336832/)

## Post

For the last 3 months, I've been preparing for AI Engineer interviews almost every day.

Something unexpected happened.

The more interviews I attended, the less the discussion was about prompts and models.

The conversation kept coming back to engineering.

Topics that repeatedly came up:
✓ FastAPI and API design
✓ Microservices and distributed systems
✓ CI/CD pipelines
✓ Docker and Kubernetes
✓ System design
✓ Evaluation and observability
✓ RAG architecture trade-offs
✓ Agent orchestration and state management
✓ Failure handling and retries

What surprised me most was this:
Many companies are not looking for someone who can simply call an LLM API.
They are looking for engineers who can build reliable systems around AI.
The model is often the smallest part of the discussion.

The larger questions are:
→ How do you evaluate it?
→ How do you monitor it?
→ How do you prevent failures?
→ How do you control costs?
→ How do you deploy it safely?

Coming from a backend engineering background, this was a reassuring realization.
The software engineering fundamentals I've built over the last decade are still highly relevant in the AI era.

AI engineering feels less like a completely new field and more like software engineering evolving to include intelligent components.

Curious to hear from others:
What topic comes up most often in your AI interviews or projects?
