𝐃𝐚𝐲 11/30: 𝐌𝐀𝐄 𝐯𝐬 𝐑𝐌𝐒𝐄 🎯

Interview question: 
"𝘠𝘰𝘶'𝘳𝘦 𝘣𝘶𝘪𝘭𝘥𝘪𝘯𝘨 𝘢 𝘩𝘰𝘶𝘴𝘦 𝘱𝘳𝘪𝘤𝘦 𝘱𝘳𝘦𝘥𝘪𝘤𝘵𝘪𝘰𝘯 𝘮𝘰𝘥𝘦𝘭.
𝘚𝘩𝘰𝘶𝘭𝘥 𝘺𝘰𝘶 𝘰𝘱𝘵𝘪𝘮𝘪𝘻𝘦 𝘧𝘰𝘳 𝘔𝘈𝘌 𝘰𝘳 𝘙𝘔𝘚𝘌?"

Most candidates say: 
"𝘙𝘔𝘚𝘌 𝘪𝘴 𝘴𝘵𝘢𝘯𝘥𝘢𝘳𝘥 𝘧𝘰𝘳 𝘳𝘦𝘨𝘳𝘦𝘴𝘴𝘪𝘰𝘯 𝘱𝘳𝘰𝘣𝘭𝘦𝘮𝘴, 𝘴𝘰 𝘐'𝘭𝘭 𝘶𝘴𝘦 𝘵𝘩𝘢𝘵."

❌ 𝐓𝐡𝐞 𝐛𝐥𝐢𝐧𝐝 𝐬𝐩𝐨𝐭:

RMSE (Root Mean Squared Error) squares the errors before averaging. 
This means:
→ A $100K error contributes 100x more than a $10K error
→ It heavily punishes large mistakes
→ A few outliers can dominate your entire metric

MAE (Mean Absolute Error) treats all errors equally:
→ A $100K error is just 10x worse than a $10K error
→ More intuitive, more robust to outliers

✅ 𝐓𝐡𝐞 𝐝𝐞𝐜𝐢𝐬𝐢𝐨𝐧 𝐟𝐫𝐚𝐦𝐞𝐰𝐨𝐫𝐤:

Use RMSE when:
→ Large errors are disproportionately bad 
 (e.g., predicting drug dosage—big mistakes are dangerous)
→ Your data has few outliers
→ You want the model to "fear" big mistakes

Use MAE when:
→ All errors are roughly equal in cost
→ Your data has outliers 
 (real estate often does—mansions skew RMSE)
→ You want interpretability 
 (MAE = average dollars off)
