# Most system design interviews don’t fail because of missing knowledge.

> **Source:** [Raul Junco](https://www.linkedin.com/in/ACoAAAESAkkB0M4J5NAS2NPh19K4SCYdD3jF3-s) · Simplifying System Design · 6mo

> [View post on LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7419726433306120193/)

## Post

Most system design interviews don’t fail because of missing knowledge.
They fail because people can’t recall the basics under pressure.

That’s why I put this together.

These aren’t trick questions.
They’re the muscle memory of backend engineering:

• scaling up vs scaling out
• SQL vs NoSQL trade-offs
• queues vs pub/sub
• idempotency, retries, and failure modes
• sharding, caching, and rate limiting
• fan-out strategies and exactly-once myths

When design questions come up, these concepts should feel automatic, like checking mirrors before changing lanes.

Save it.
Revisit it.
Use it to stress-test your understanding.

Because in real systems and real interviews, fundamentals decide everything.
