🚨 You're missing years of ML Engineering experience - you can fix it in just 1 hour 🚨
 
GPU compute is arguably the least accessible aspect of machine learning -- primarily because of its cost. Training large language models (LLMs) remains a rare privilege. If you're in academia, you're likely experimenting in-depth with smaller-scale models due to resource constraints. On the other hand, if you're in industry working on large-scale training, you're operating in environments where every command can cost tens or even hundreds of thousands of dollars. In such high-stakes settings, there's little room for hands-on exploration or deep experimentation.
 
This single video by Justin Johnson breaks down concepts that typically take years of hands-on experience with large-scale model training to fully understand.
 
🔧 What you'll learn:
 
* How training scaled from 1 GPU to 10,000+ (e.g. Llama 3's 24k GPU cluster)
 
* Inside the Nvidia H100: compute cores, HBM, tensor cores & more
 
* GPU clusters explained: from single GPU to full-scale racks & pods
 
* Degrees of Parallelism:
 
 - Data Parallelism (DP): Splits training data across GPUs
 - Fully Sharded Data Parallelism (FSDP): Divides model weights, gradients, and optimizer states across GPUs
 - Activation Checkpointing: Recomputes activations during the backward pass instead of storing them
 - Context Parallelism: Splits computation of long sequences
 - Pipeline Parallelism: Divides network layers across different GPUs
 - Tensor Parallelism: Splits the weight matrices across GPUs
 
* MFU (Model Flops Utilization) -- the #1 metric to track GPU efficiency
