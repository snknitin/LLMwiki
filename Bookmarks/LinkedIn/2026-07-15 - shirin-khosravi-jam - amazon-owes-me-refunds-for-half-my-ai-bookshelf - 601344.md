Most LLM books fall into two buckets: 
dense theory you never finish, or API tutorials outdated in six months.

"50 ML Projects To Understand LLMs" by Mike X Cohen, PhD is neither. 

Instead of teaching you to build or deploy, it teaches you to open the black box: hidden states, attention patterns, and embeddings become data you analyze yourself.

50 hands-on projects in Python and PyTorch, covering:

1️⃣ Tokenization and embedding spaces
→ Cosine similarity, semantic axes, analogy vectors

2️⃣ Output logits and perplexity
→ Softmax distributions and language biases

3️⃣ Attention mechanisms
→ QKV weights, head ablation, activation patching

4️⃣ MLP circuits and interpretability
→ Neuron tuning, probing, causal tracing

Every project comes with full and partial solution notebooks, so you actually practice instead of just reading.

Requirements: basic Python, some ML familiarity. No LLM background needed.

Remember:
Architectures will keep evolving. 
The skill of investigating them won't expire.
