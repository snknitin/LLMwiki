Design brief (from the ui-craft skill; follow it, don't go read the skill library):
- Design from the domain, not from a style. First answer: what does the person come to do here, how do they feel arriving, what shape is the data, and which two things does this kind of app reward (money: precision and calm; habits and health: momentum and low friction; news and feeds: recency and hierarchy; learning: focus and visible progress; ops and agents: awareness and trust; people: warmth and memory; research: rigour and traceability). Every choice below must serve those two.
- Generic UI comes from a generic brief. Before any HTML, write each option's brief: the person and the moment; the domain line; surface (Operate tool, Persuade page, Read document); the concept as a structure from the subject's world that decides the layout, not texture on a generic page (a 24-hour timeline hung on market closes, a dictionary page filling in, a departure board of due cards); 3 defaults you reject; a strict palette of 4-6 named hex values with roles and the accent's one job; named type stacks and a scale; the composition in one line; one depth strategy; one signature element plus one interaction that enacts it.
- Options differ in concept and layout, not colour. Draw from different families: instruments and machines, transit and wayfinding, maps and charts, science and specimens, games and scoreboards, broadcast and media, software-native (terminal, spreadsheet, precise tool UI), architecture and signage, craft and movements (Swiss, Bauhaus, Memphis, Art Deco), print and paper. AI-designed apps keep landing on paper, ledger, stamp or notebook themes: use at most one paper or print-document concept per round, and only when the subject really is paper.
- Avoid the current AI looks: cream background with a serif and terracotta accent; near-black with one acid accent; identical rounded cards with the same soft shadow; aurora or glass blobs; a centred hero with three cards; one italic accent word in a headline; purple or indigo gradients; emoji icons; a blinking status dot.
- Let the layout explain: no eyebrow caption over a heading, no tagline, no sentence explaining a panel, no instructions on screen. Buttons are 1-3 words starting with a verb. Keep units, amounts and what a save covers. Labels sit above their values at full width.
- Readable and ranked: reading text at least 16px; 2 families, 2 weights, 3 text tones; one filled primary action per section; the most important thing top-left and largest; numbers right-aligned in tabular figures; spacing on a 4/8 scale, tight inside groups and wide between them; nothing floating without a shared edge.
- Real, plentiful content: names, numbers and dates as this app would show them, enough to fill the first screen; designed hover, focus and empty states where visible; works at 390px with nothing clipped.
- Colour has a job: neutral surface layers, one accent ramp, semantic red/amber/green; no colour as decoration. Motion only to answer an action, under 300ms.
- The first frame must already look finished and dense. Look at your screenshots, fix the worst problem, re-shoot.
